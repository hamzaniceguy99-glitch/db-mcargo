How we work

A haulier that explains the liability limit first

DB-M Cargo is a company incorporated under Belgian law, registered with the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises under enterprise number 0840.029.106, based at 4 Schaapschuur in 1790 Affligem and operating since October 2011. Road haulage for hire or reward requires access to the occupation under European rules: a community licence, a designated transport manager holding the certificate of professional competence, good repute and proven financial standing.

The thing worth knowing before you ship anything is how little the law pays out. Under the CMR Convention, which governs international carriage by road, compensation for lost or damaged goods is capped at 8.33 special drawing rights per kilogram of gross weight, whatever the goods were actually worth. A tonne of circuit boards and a tonne of gravel are compensated identically. We put that in writing with every quote and recommend ad valorem cover where it is needed. We also plan to the driving and rest-time rules and will refuse a delivery slot that could only be met by exceeding them, and we do not carry dangerous goods at all.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

What does CMR actually pay if my goods are lost?

8.33 special drawing rights per kilogram of gross weight short, plus the carriage charges. That is a weight-based figure with no relation to value, so a light high-value consignment is badly under-compensated. If your goods are worth meaningfully more than that, arrange ad valorem or goods-in-transit cover before they move; we will say so in the quote.

Can you collect at four and deliver first thing the next morning?

Only if legal driving and rest times reach it. If they do not, we will tell you the earliest slot that does rather than take the booking and hope. A driver over hours risks a fine and an immobilised vehicle, which turns your urgent load into a much later one.

Can you take a pallet of paint or aerosols?

No. Those fall under the ADR rules for dangerous goods and we do not carry them. If a consignment turns out to contain them undeclared it will be refused at loading, because the exposure is not only ours — it travels with every other consignment on the vehicle.

Can you store our chilled stock?

No. Temperature-controlled food storage requires authorisation from the Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain, which we do not hold. Ambient warehousing we can do, with an insured value agreed in writing and stock recorded in and out.

Will you guarantee the delivery time?

No, and nor can anyone honestly. We commit to the collection and to a realistic transit, and we tell you promptly when something moves. Traffic, weather, border formalities, a customs inspection or a consignee with narrow receiving hours will all delay a vehicle, and none of them are within a haulier's control.

Why do you ask for the value if liability is by weight?

Precisely because liability is by weight. Knowing the value is how we work out the gap between what CMR would pay and what you would actually lose, which is the information you need to decide whether to insure. A carrier who never asks is not protecting you from the question.
